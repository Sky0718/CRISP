Finite DawnRank graph-construction controls

This analysis asks whether allocation differences persist across matched
edge counts and alternative score thresholds. It compares the complete
specified set of network constructions and retains changes in both
directions. These are explanatory, post-exposure comparisons.

Population and recurrence
The population comprises 85 colorectal models from 83 donors, 7,399 common
genes, both consumed-evidence states H0/H1 and each complete mutation query.
The offered budget is ten, with fractional allocation at exact-score cutoff
ties. The recurrence uses R 4.5.2, Matrix 1.7.4, mu=3, epsilon=1e-12 and
maxit=10000. Deterministic seed references 104729, 130363, 155921, 196613 and
228017 are aliases of the same DawnRank solution rather than replicate fits.
The reference states comprise two graphs x two evidence states x 85 models,
giving 340 score vectors.

Graph construction
Release-specific physical-network and protein-info sources are mapped through
literal preferred names. Ambiguous names and loops are excluded. Equal scored
reciprocal records collapse to canonical undirected edges on the common
7,399-gene domain, including isolates. Original strictly >700 graphs contain
119,423 old-release edges and 65,783 new-release edges.

Ten control graphs are defined:
1. Three old-release graphs containing its 65,783 highest-score edges.
2. Three new-release graphs containing its 119,423 highest-score edges.
3. Old and new graphs at each strict threshold >650 and >750, giving four
   further graphs.

For each edge-count target, all edges above the boundary score are included.
The required number of boundary-tied edges is selected in ascending SHA-256
order of seed + TAB + canonical gene1 + TAB + canonical gene2. The three tie
seeds are 271828, 314159 and 173205. All three selections are reported,
including their range. They represent boundary-tie choices, not biological
replicates. Count matching also changes edge identities and degree
distributions, so the contrast is not an isolated causal density intervention.

Each graph defines a symmetric binary adjacency, column-normalised propagation
matrix and degree/(degree+3) damping. These graph objects are distinct from
the wider weighted backgrounds used by PRODIGY.

States and contrasts
Ten control graphs x two evidence states x 85 models define 1,700 full score
vectors. Ten control degree rankings x 85 models define 850 model rankings,
alongside the two reference degree graph states. Degree is H-independent.

The nine graph pairs are the original >700 pair, three matched-65,783 pairs,
three matched-119,423 pairs, the >650 pair and the >750 pair. Their two H
backgrounds define 18 DawnRank contrasts. Nine degree contrasts give 27
paired contrasts in total. H backgrounds and tie seeds remain distinct
conditions. Complete graph/state levels accompany every contrast.

Endpoints and labels
Endpoints cover ALL, all four exclusive core classes and all five exclusive
cancer-role classes: returned allocation R, measured-positive allocation H,
measured-zero allocation, unknown allocation, and pooled measured-positive
fraction where measurement is complete and the denominator is positive.
Individual donor values accompany population summaries. Direct new-minus-old
sharp bounds combine shared sample-gene coefficients before optimising
unknown binary labels. Shared-core and oncogene-only summaries sit within
the complete category partitions.

Functional labels are excluded from graph construction and ranking.
Allocations, the 27 coefficient maps and their selected identities define the
label query. Calls use the same mapped Table 5 source and consistent
selected-label caches, distinguishing measured zero, measured one,
explicit-null and source-absent states. Source exposure makes these
explanatory comparisons rather than independent biological validation.

Numerical checks
Fixtures cover threshold equality, score ties, reciprocal consistency,
mapping ambiguity, exact target counts, tie hashes, normalisation orientation,
isolates, direct-solve agreement, fractional cutoff ties, short queries,
unequal model-per-donor weights and shared-label cancellation. Source
ordering, complete populations and deterministic seed references are bound
to the inputs.

Every top-ten cutoff is assessed with the residual/contraction error bound.
Nonconverged vectors and uncertified cutoffs remain explicit failures.
Independent Python/SciPy reconstruction uses the exported binary64 matrices
and q, compares complete query orders and allocations, and independently
reconciles state and contrast aggregates.

Output coverage
Outputs contain native vectors, graph edges, boundary counts, full query
rankings, contrast coefficients, selected-label identities and values,
source bindings and numerical comparisons. The analysis is finite and
descriptive; it introduces no resampling family or P values.
